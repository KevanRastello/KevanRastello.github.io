Regular or methods paper? Test 1: is the take-home message about the system or a procedure? Test 2: would the paper still matter with a different study system?

## Block B: the answer first

**B1.** Your conclusions: at most five, one sentence each, no "however".

*Methods paper:* What the method does better than existing ones, with numbers.

**B2.** Your figures (about five at most) and tables (about four), in the order that best tells the story. One line each: what should the reader see?

**B3.** The results each conclusion needs, with direction, size and figure. Then, separately: the results that do not fit the conclusions.

*Methods paper:* Performance results (accuracy, error rates, sensitivity) and the comparison with alternatives.

**B4.** The take-home message in one sentence: not what you found, but what it changes. Leave it a day, then revise.

**B5.** The central question and its answer, in two sentences.

## Block C: the question and the argument

**C1.** The original question and predictions, as they stood before you saw the results (from your notes or proposal). Mark any that changed, and any new ones: those are reported as exploratory.

*Methods paper:* The performance claims you set out to test.

> Your answer

**C2.** Numbered predictions, P1, P2…, in the order the paper will answer them.

> Your answer

**C3.** The big question your field cares about, and your pitch: resolves a controversy / a gap that blocks a wider goal / blocks a practical problem / a model system for a wider field / a new path to a hard goal.

*Methods paper:* The task researchers need to do, in any system.

> Your answer

**C4.** What the reader must know first: two to four points, each with its key references.

*Methods paper:* How the task is done now, and where that falls short.

> Your answer

**C5.** The gap and why it matters, without "no studies have" or "to increase our understanding".

*Methods paper:* The gap in methods, stated without your study system.

> Your answer

**C6.** For each prediction: the data and analysis needed, and only those.

*Methods paper:* The method's components and assumptions, the simulation design, the alternatives compared, the case study.

> Your answer

**C7.** The story in three lines: And (context), But (gap), Therefore (what you did and found).

> Your answer

## Block D: meaning and limits

**D1.** For each conclusion: does it agree with, extend or contradict earlier work? Name the papers.

> Your answer

**D2.** The limitations that could change a conclusion, and by how much.

*Methods paper:* The method's assumptions, and when someone should not use it.

> Your answer

**D3.** What changes for management or theory?

*Methods paper:* How to implement it; where the code and data are.

> Your answer

**D4.** A working title that states the finding: short, no question mark.

*Methods paper:* A title that says what the method does.

> Your answer

**D5.** What you are leaving out, and why: not relevant, redundant, or a separate paper. Conflicting results never go here.

> Your answer

## Four gates before drafting

- [ ] **Message.** B4 is one sentence, states an implication, and passes the Step 0 test. You left it a day and revised it. Your reader from A1 could repeat it.
- [ ] **Honesty.** C1 came from your notes, not your results. Every prediction not in C1 is labelled exploratory. B3 lists the results that do not fit, and D5 hides none of them.
- [ ] **Order.** The predictions in C2, figures in B2, analyses in C6 and comparisons in D1 all follow the same order.
- [ ] **Budget.** The paragraph count fits the A2 budget. If it is over, cut a prediction or move it to the supplement before writing.
